One pattern, three times. Each family passed its own suite while broken, because the author wrote the code and the tests in one pass. The tests then encode the author's belief, not the source. Reading the code only re-confirms that belief. Driving the real kernel from a fresh save is the thing that breaks the loop, and it is now required in every verification brief.

Four decisions

1 · Skeletal Horror wish lists cost one button press

The real gate is the Rag and Bone Man and Fur 'n Seek post-quest wish lists. In the period each one is a long bone-collection errand. AnkiScape has no wish-list state, because the quest-parity thread owns it.

2 · Who is credited for a purple-portal crossfire kill?

Matches resolve by crossfire: the rest of the arena wears your rival down and someone lands the last blow. In the two free-for-all portals that someone is a stranger, so it scores against you. In a clan battle the fighters around you are clan mates.

Also worth knowing. A purple win was not observed at combat level 10 across 400 matches, and becomes reachable near level 30. You play from level 1, so you will meet that stretch before you meet the win.

3 · How many reviews is one cavern visit worth?

The period timer is exact: your visit lasts your Quest Points in ticks, capped at three minutes. No source says how many answered cards that is, because the period game had no such unit.

4 · Quest-awarded clues bypass the one-clue rule

The drop paths now enforce one clue in total. One quest in the catalog awards an elite clue as an item reward, and quest rewards skip the ownership filter, so a player can end that quest holding two.
