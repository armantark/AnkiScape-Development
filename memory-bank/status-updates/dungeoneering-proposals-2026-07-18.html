What is Dungeoneering in AnkiScape?

The source in one breath: Daemonheim is a 60-floor dungeon skill. You walk in with nothing, scavenge and craft gear from what the floor gives you, get past locked doors and skill gates, kill the floor boss, and walk out with Dungeoneering XP and tokens. Nothing you found comes out with you. Then you do it again, deeper.

Where we stand:
  • Every other activity is now design-closed. Dungeoneering is the last one, and it's the capstone skill — the one that uses everything else.
  • Six technical sub-questions are queued behind this page. They all become obvious once you pick a model here, so pick the shape first.
  • All models obey the house rules: one successful review = one meaningful beat, nothing runs on wall-clock, Again advances only the world.

Six models — click to select

1 · The Roguelike FloorRECOMMENDED

A floor is a little dungeon map you actually explore, one room per review.

A review is
One room: fight it, skill it, or unlock it — "Cleared the frozen room: cut the roots, cooked the eel, beat the ranger."
Persists
The whole floor, indefinitely. Come back tomorrow, it's waiting.
Death
Respawn at floor start; the end-of-floor payout shrinks. Never erases the floor.
Payout
XP + tokens when the boss falls, scaled by depth, deaths, and completeness.

Keeps: exploration, keys, branching, scarcity, binds, complexity, prestige. Loses: only the literal map screen.

2 · The Fresh-Start Run

Same engine as #1, tuned so every floor feels like starting a new save file.

A review is
Same room beats — but floors are shorter and scarcity is cranked: gear hunger drives every choice.
Persists
The floor, same as #1.
Death
Same as #1 but stings more, since gear is scarcer.
Payout
Same, with more frequent floor completions.

Keeps: everything #1 keeps, plus the "fresh account" thrill named in the packet thesis. Loses: longer strategic floors.

3 · The Boss Ladder

No map. Each floor is a fixed short arc: gear up → pass the gate → fight the boss.

A review is
One of 3–5 fixed beats per floor, always in the same shape.
Persists
Your rung on the 60-floor ladder.
Death
Retry the boss beat; payout shrinks.
Payout
XP + tokens per rung, bosses get real mechanics via the multi-enemy contract.

Keeps: depth ladder, bosses, binds. Loses: exploration, keys, branching, room variety — the dungeon part of the dungeon.

4 · The Descent

One continuous dive with no floor boundaries — go deep until you die or leave.

A review is
One room, but rooms just keep coming, harder and harder.
Persists
Your current depth and kit, until the run ends.
Death
Ends the run — that IS the game. Payout scales with depth reached.
Payout
Banked on exit; deeper = disproportionately more.

Keeps: tension, scarcity. Loses: the actual floor/prestige structure — this invents a mode the 2012 game didn't have. Weakest source claim.

5 · The Expedition Campaign

Model #1 wrapped in chapters: Frozen floors, then Abandoned, then Furnished… like a story campaign.

A review is
Same room beats as #1.
Persists
Floor + a visible campaign map of themes, milestones, and journal pages.
Death
Same as #1.
Payout
Same, plus chapter-completion milestones (sagas, journals) surfaced as campaign beats.

Keeps: everything #1 keeps + long-arc motivation. Loses: nothing — but it's #1 plus a whole presentation layer. Biggest build.

6 · The Training Hall

The honest anti-model: Dungeoneering as plain skill rows — "clear rooms," earn XP and a token trickle.

A review is
An abstract room-clear row, like a Smithing target.
Persists
Nothing but your XP and tokens.
Death
Doesn't exist.
Payout
Flat drip, cadence-calibrated.

Keeps: the XP curve and token shop. Loses: the entire skill identity — this is what "just ship something" looks like, included so the floor of the decision is visible.
