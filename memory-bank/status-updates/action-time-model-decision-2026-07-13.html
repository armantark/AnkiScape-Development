Decision required · Pre-implementation · Blocks Thieving

One card, one action — or one card, one hour?

Thieving surfaced a contradiction that has been latent in AnkiScape since the beginning, and it cannot ship without you resolving it. Claude and GPT-5.6 Sol each attacked it independently as a TRIZ contradiction. Sol found a fatal flaw in Claude's answer. This is the fork.

01The contradiction

Stated crisply, in the form TRIZ requires — because if you can't state it crisply, you don't have a TRIZ problem.

Improving XP/HOUR PARITY degrades ONE REVIEW = ONE ACTION ...and improving the axiom degrades parity.

RuneScape was balanced in XP per hour. AnkiScape pays XP per action. Those agree only if every action takes the same real time. Mostly they do (~3s), which is why pickpocketing already comes out correctly ordered. They diverge violently wherever dead time exists — real seconds that elapse without being an action.

The Ideal Final Result

Every review visibly performs exactly one understandable action or attempt. Authentic XP is awarded unchanged. Sourced XP/hour ratios emerge from cadence. No clock, no conversion constant, and no hand-tuning is ever exposed.

The time model eliminates itself — into the success roll that already exists. In the real game, "this action is slow" and "this action's roll usually fails" are the same sentence. A runite rock is slow because the per-tick roll fails. mining_data.py has been silently doing duration modelling this whole time.

02The formula — and why my first one was wrong

I proposed reconstructing the rate from respawn timers. Sol pointed out that we already have the sourced XP/hour, and reconstructing it from "roughly 2–3 seconds" discards better data. He's right, and it matters: the correct form makes the roll derived from sources rather than tuned by me.

CLAUDE'S PROPOSAL — REJECTED
p = min(1, action_seconds / respawn_seconds)

Reconstructs one possible cause of the rate, from two weak numbers, when the rate itself is sourced. And the min(1, …) silently caps the vegetable stall at 12,000 XP/hr when the wiki says 18,000 — hiding an impossible mapping instead of resolving it.

SOL'S FORM — ADOPTED
actions_per_hour = sourced_XP_per_hour / XP_per_action actions_per_review = actions_per_hour / R

Both numerator and denominator are pinned to 2012 sources. The only free parameter in the entire game becomes R — reviews per balancing-hour. One global dial. The invention budget goes to zero.

02bThe correction — Mining is already inverted

I told the owner in chat that gathering skills already satisfied XP/hour parity "for free", because their low_chance/high_chance rolls are period-authentic. That was wrong. The rolls are not used raw. Every gathering skill routes them through an invented adapter, and the adapter inverts the skill.

logic_pure.py:388 def normalize_review_success_probability_pure(source_chance, ...): """Adapt source tick odds to one AnkiScape review-action roll.""" return min(cap, minimum + sqrt(source) * scale) REVIEW_SUCCESS_MINIMUM = 0.20 REVIEW_SUCCESS_SCALE = 0.72 REVIEW_SUCCESS_CAP = 0.95

A 0.20 floor plus a square root massively inflates rare actions. Rare actions are exactly the ones whose real XP/hour is bad — that is why they're rare. So the adapter systematically over-rewards precisely the content the real game made unattractive. Shipped, live, in Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing and Hunter (logic_pure.py:7343, 7409, 7488, 7643).

The damage is proportional to how rare the action is — and that predicts which skills broke, before you look. Near p=1 the square root is nearly the identity and does no harm. As p→0 it inflates without bound. Measured across every adapter skill by tools/xp_rate_audit.py:

04Sol's hits on my proposal

I asked it to attack, not to agree. Five landed; two I reject.

The min(1, …) cap hides an impossible mappingFatal

The vegetable stall does 1,800 successful steals/hour — faster than one action per review at any sane R. My cap silently swallowed the overflow. This is the hit that kills the proposal: it means the fastest stall in the game cannot be represented under the axiom unless R ≥ 1,800, and that choice drags the pace of the entire game with it.

A memoryless roll is not a refractory cooldownReal

A 180-second restock is deterministic. A geometric random variable is not. With p = 1/60 there's a 36.5% chance the stall is still unavailable after 60 attempts, and a 1.7% chance it's back instantly. Neither resembles a restock. Fix: deterministic cadence with fractional residuals — a gem theft due every 88.9 reviews alternates 88- and 89-review intervals. No second lottery on top of a rate we already know exactly.

The stall-rotation exploit is unaddressedReal

There is no location layer, so travel is free. If eighteen stalls each hold an independent cooldown, the player round-robins all of them and collects every one as it comes up — turning the throttle into a free XP buffet. This needs an explicit rule and I had not thought of it.

Combat cannot be deferred, because XP is pinnedFatal

Combat's 30-second burst pays roughly 8× what an equivalent hour of skilling pays. A later "rebalancing pass" cannot fix it by lowering XP — XP/action is frozen by the parity contract. The only lever is cadence, which is precisely the architectural decision I was proposing to postpone. Deferring it doesn't buy time; it just guarantees a migration.

Double-charging the failure rollReal

If availability and the theft-success roll are applied independently, the sourced rate gets suppressed twice. Availability must be calibrated against expected XP per opportunity, not per success.

"An empty stall isn't an action, it's a state observation"Rejected

Mining has failed on ~93% of runite reviews since launch and nobody has ever called that a broken axiom. "You try the stall; it's bare" is an attempt with an outcome. The axiom always meant one action or attempt — Sol's own restatement concedes this.

Wall-clock simulationRejected

Sol's Candidate 2. Makes leaving Anki open progression-relevant and rewards slow reviewers. Destroys a more important axiom than the one it saves.

05The fork

All three keep sourced XP untouched. They differ in what they're willing to spend.

Option A · Already what we're doing

Thieving now, stalls approximate

COST: 0 · DEBT: NOW FIVE SKILLS

Ship the availability roll as first proposed. Accept the cap, the variance, the rotation hole — and join Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing and Hunter, which are already inverted.

  • Thieving ships this week
  • Stalls knowingly ~50× wrong
  • Combat stays ~15× over-rewarded
  • Runite keeps out-earning iron
  • A fifth broken skill on a stack of four
Buys a week. The bill compounds.
Option B · Recommended

Build the cadence kernel first

COST: ONE WORKSTREAM · DEBT: 0

Sol's Candidate 4. One deterministic cadence layer between reviews and skills, with two kinds of state: a resource lock ("the stall is bare, you are free") and an actor lock ("you are still committed to this fight").

  • Cadence derived from sourced XP/hour — nothing invented
  • Fixes combat's 8× in the same change
  • Thieving stalls become correct, not approximate
  • Every future skill lands on a correct foundation
  • Combat must expose internal events rather than one 50-tick payout
  • Re-verify the full 2,100-test baseline
Pay once, now, while Thieving is the forcing function.
Option C · Split

Thieving minus stalls

COST: LOW · DEBT: MEDIUM

Ship pickpocketing, blackjacking, chests, doors, wall safes now — every family whose rate limiter is a roll, not dead time. Hold stalls for the kernel.

  • Nothing shipped is wrong
  • ~80% of Thieving lands now
  • Violates "all training sources should exist"
  • Combat's 8× still unaddressed
The honest compromise, if B is too much at once.

06Settled between Claude and Sol — no decision needed from you

Two rounds of adversarial back-and-forth closed these. They are recorded so you can overrule any of them, not so you have to ratify them.

✅ R = 1000, with two named caps

Sol opened at R≥1800 (nothing may be capped), I argued the modal action, and it conceded — once you accept explicitly declared caps, nothing forces R to cover the fastest action. Vegetable and baker's stalls declare cap_one_action; validation fails if a third overflow ever appears without one. If caps proliferate, reopen R.

✅ Stall rotation: routes, not a free-for-all

I proposed rotation yields the sum of stall rates. Sol rejected it and is right: summing 18 sourced single-stall rates invents an aggregate no source states. Only the active route advances; inactive routes freeze. An ardougne_stall_circuit route may exist only if a period source gives its aggregate rate.

✅ Catch fires after a successful theft

The sourced stall rate already assumes the theft succeeds. So the catch may deal damage, take coins, and log a counter — but it may never cancel XP, confiscate the loot, or consume a future review. One roll, not two: 15% if a guard applies, 5% if only the owner does.

✅ Deterministic cadence, never a lottery

Integer residual accumulator, no floats persisted. The gem stall at 81/4000 emits thefts at exact 49- and 50-review intervals. Switching away freezes the residual; switching back resumes it. No dry streaks, no lucky doubles on a rate we already know exactly.

R = 1000 has three supports — the 3.6s blackjack cycle, the overflow caps, and now the period record's own action rates. R = 1800 has none. The one open Claude/Sol disagreement, settled on evidence neither had.

COMBAT — MEASURED, NOT ESTIMATED
moss_giant 309.8 xp/review -> 37,172 xp/hr @ R=120 hill_giant 304.8 xp/review -> 36,573 xp/hr @ R=120 iron (mine) 32.2 xp/review -> ~35,000 xp/hr @ R=1035

The combat engine is faithful. 37k XP/hr on moss giants is a plausible period rate. The simulation is not the bug.

What's wrong is the exchange rate. Moss giants pay 309.8 XP/review; iron pays 32.2 — for the same real hourly rate. One combat card buys 5.5×–8.6× more game time than any other card.

Combat's R = 120 is a definition (50 ticks × 0.6s). Every other skill's R is derived. Proving this needs no combat XP/hour source at all — it's pure tick-to-review conversion. Which is exactly why it cannot be fixed later by lowering XP: XP/action is frozen by the parity contract. Cadence is the only lever.

07The one thing that is actually yours

This stopped being a Thieving question the moment I found the adapter.

When I wrote the first draft of this page, the pitch was: "stalls need a fix, and combat is 15× off, so let's build the kernel while Thieving is the forcing function." That was already a decent argument. It is now a much blunter one.

Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing and Hunter are shipped, live, and inverted — runite out-earns iron, when reality says the opposite by 3–4×. Combat pays ~15× an equivalent hour of skilling. Thieving's stalls would be wrong by ~50×. These are not three bugs; they are one bug, wearing three coats: XP per review was never tied to XP per hour, and nothing in the codebase ever made it so.

So "Option A — ship it broken" is no longer really on the table, because we are already shipping it broken. The honest choice is between fixing it now, with Thieving as the forcing function and a full spec already written — or shipping a fifth broken skill on top of four, and paying for all five later.

What I need from you is not the mechanism — Sol and I have that. It's the appetite. This is a real workstream: a cadence kernel, a combat engine split (persistent tick budget instead of one 50-tick burst), a save-schema migration, and re-verification of the 2,100-test baseline. That is not a Thieving-sized ask, and per your own rule I'm not going to pretend otherwise or start it on my own.
